Circuit/Schematic Diagram Recognition and Conversion to Machine-Readable Formats: A 2024–
2026 Survey with Emphasis on VLMs/MLLMs
TL;DR
    SPICE netlist is the dominant target, and the field has converged on a clear verdict: hybrid pipelines (object detection + explicit
    wire/net reasoning + LLM serialization/repair) still decisively beat end-to-end MLLMs for full connectivity. Pipeline systems
    report 80–97% netlist-level accuracy (Image2Net 80.77% success / 0.116 NED; SINA 96.67%), whereas off-the-shelf MLLMs are
    strong on component perception but collapse on global connectivity.
    The perception-vs-connectivity gap is now quantified: on AMSbench, Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches 94% component-type classification
    but topology NED remains far from ground truth (0.91) and “connection identification” naming drops to ~0.60; on CircuitSense,
    Gemini-2.5-Pro’s accuracy falls from 80.71% on curated multiple-choice to 19.06% on synthetic equation derivation (~61-point
    collapse). This is the direct analog of the perception-vs-global-structure gap familiar from table-structure recognition.
    For a Document-AI practitioner, the actionable recipe is: detector (YOLO/DETR/Swin) for components + designators,
    deterministic net reconstruction (CCL/Hough + crossing-vs-junction disambiguation), then a fine-tuned code-LLM/VLM
    (Qwen2.5/3-VL, InternVL) served on vLLM for serialization, error-repair, and bulk corpus/synthetic-data generation — with
    graph-isomorphism / netlist-edit-distance validation in the loop.
Key Findings
 1. Output formats. SPICE netlist is the near-universal target for analog/mixed-signal (AMS) work; graph/adjacency (heterogeneous
    graphs, pin-level graphs, GraphML/JSON) is the internal representation of choice for GNN methods and validation; there is no
    widely adopted “circuit-XML” benchmark standard — CGHD ships PASCAL-VOC XML for bounding boxes, not circuit topology,
    and EDIF/KiCad/ .asc formats appear as tool outputs rather than recognition targets. Verilog/RTL is a digital target largely disjoint
    from the schematic-recognition literature (it is generated from specs, not recognized from images). “Compact netlist” DSLs are an
    emerging token-efficiency play (ChatSCH’s Compact Netlist reduces token overhead by ~85%; AnalogGenie’s Eulerian pin-
    sequence).
 2. Three problem regimes with distinct communities. (a) Printed/typeset AMS schematics from textbooks/papers — the EDA-driven
    mainstream (AMSNet, Masala-CHAI, Image2Net, Netlistify, SINA, AnalogSeeker). (b) Hand-drawn/handwritten —
    education/digitization-driven, centered on DFKI’s CGHD and the ICDAR/document-analysis community (Digitize-HCD, JUHCCR-
    v1, Modular Graph Extraction). (c) Board-level/PCB and digital — smaller, newer (SINA’s PCB support, ChatSCH/DesignSCH-CoT
    board-level).
  3. The empirical MLLM result is consistent across benchmarks: perception (component detection/classification) is largely solved by
     frontier closed models; connectivity reconstruction and downstream symbolic/design reasoning are not.
Details
1. Output formats and token-length implications
SPICE netlist (dominant). The canonical machine-readable target. Each device line ( M0 1 2 4 4 pch , R0 2 0 1k ) binds a component
to its nets. This is what AMSNet, Auto-SPICE/Masala-CHAI, Image2Net, Netlistify, SINA, and the AMSbench “perception” task all target.
SPICE is attractive because it is directly simulable (ngspice), enabling simulation-equivalence checking and closed-loop repair.
Graph/adjacency representations. Internally, most robust methods reconstruct a graph first and serialize to SPICE second. Variants: (i)
component-nodes + net-nodes bipartite/heterogeneous graphs (Image2Net “transforms the circuit netlist into an equivalent
heterogeneous graph” for its NED metric; GAT-parsing uses link prediction over detected components); (ii) pin-level graphs where each
node is a device pin (AnalogGenie), sequentialized into an Eulerian trail for compact autoregressive generation; (iii) subgraph-basis DAGs
(CktGNN’s two-level nested GNN over a known subgraph basis). JSON/GraphML are used as interchange but no single schema
dominates.
XML. Important negative finding for the Document-AI reader: there is no established circuit-topology-XML recognition benchmark.
CGHD annotations are PASCAL-VOC XML encoding symbol bounding boxes, rotation, and text strings — i.e., detection ground truth, not
a netlist. EDIF and KiCad/EESchema/Xschem formats exist as EDA tool I/O (Schemato targets LTspice .asc ; automatic schematic-
generation tools emit Xschem/KiCad), but recognition papers do not evaluate against them as canonical targets. This contrasts sharply
with document AI, where formats like ALTO/PAGE-XML and HTML-table markup are standardized recognition targets.
Verilog/RTL. Belongs to the digital design-generation literature (VeriGen, AutoChip, RTLLM). It is generated from natural-language specs
or synthesized, not recognized from schematic images; the schematic-recognition pipeline and RTL-generation pipeline are largely
disjoint communities that share only the “LLM for EDA” umbrella.
Compact-netlist DSLs and token length. Serialized targets have direct VLM-decoding cost. A transistor-level netlist can run to hundreds
of lines; Masala-CHAI explicitly analyzes the distribution of “#lines of SPICE code” as a difficulty axis. This motivates: (i) compact DSLs —
per ChatSCH (Wang et al., ICIC 2026, doi 10.1007/978-981-92-3441-7_32), “we construct DesignSCH-CoT, the first board-level schematic
dataset with explicit Chain-of-Thought supervision, comprising 3,781 samples in a Compact Netlist format that reduces token overhead by
nearly 85%”; and AnalogGenie’s Eulerian pin-sequence “that efficiently describes large circuit graphs”; and (ii) the practical observation
that long netlists blow past attention/positional budgets and inflate error rates — the “long-netlist token limit” open problem. For a
decoder, every extra net name and designator is more tokens to emit correctly, and a single mis-emitted net silently corrupts topology
(exact-match and simulation-equivalence both fail).
2. Approaches
Modular / pipeline methods (current SOTA for connectivity)
The dominant architecture mirrors document structure recognition: detect primitives → localize connection points → reconstruct
adjacency → serialize. Component detection ≈ cell detection; wire/net reconstruction ≈ table structure/adjacency recovery.
     AMSNet (arXiv 2405.09045; IEEE LAD 2024; Tao, Shi et al.). The seminal pipeline+dataset. Component detection (object
     detection over textbook-harvested symbols) + net detection under two assumptions: nets are solid lines, and intersecting wires
     without a junction dot are not connected. Summary drawn “from 894 schematics”; the initial public release comprised 734 netlists
     (the baseline Masala-CHAI later cites). Followed by AMSnet 2.0 (arXiv 2505.09155) adding AI segmentation for net detection and
     richer visual info, and aiming at netlist→schematic generation.
     Parsing Netlists via GAT (Hu, Zhan, Tong; Sensors 2024, 24(1):227, doi 10.3390/s24010227). Bottom-up graph encoding: an
     improved Swin-Transformer detector with a DC-PAFPN feature-fusion neck, a port-coordinate localization module, and a Graph
     Attention Network link-prediction head. Annotated a 1,200-sample component-detection set and a 3,552-sample circuit set.
     Canonical example of casting net reconstruction as edge/link prediction over a component graph — directly analogous to adjacency-
     matrix prediction in table structure recognition.
     Modular Graph Extraction (Bayer et al., arXiv 2402.11093). The hand-drawn counterpart, built on CGHD. Explicit modular
     pipeline: object detection → geometric/structural processing (junctions, wire hops) → graph assembly. Uses drafter splits (e.g.,
     drafters 21–22 for validation) that became a de-facto handwritten baseline.
     Masala-CHAI (arXiv 2411.14299; evolved from Auto-SPICE). Fully automated textbook→SPICE pipeline: (i) YOLOv8
     schematic/figure extraction from paginated PDFs, (ii) fine-tuned component detectors, (iii) deep Hough-transform priors for net
     identification, (iv) LLM prompt-tuning for netlist repair, (v) post-extraction verification via graph matching. Per Bhandari et al., it
     curated “a corpus of ∼7,500 SPICE netlists with metadata (figure captions, components, and nodes) from 10 textbooks … the largest
     curated and labeled SPICE dataset available currently,” dwarfing the AMSNet baseline of 734 netlists. A later multi-agent variant
     (Agent-1 lists components, Agent-2 traces connectivity + runs ngspice, a Judge verifies, up to 2 revisions). Earlier Auto-SPICE (arXiv
     2411.14299v1) produced ~2,100 netlists from a single sample textbook and did the GPT-4o failure-mode case study (NMOS/PMOS
     confusion, floating nets).
     Netlistify (MLCAD 2025, doi 10.1109/MLCAD65511.2025.11189145; Huang, Chen, … Po-Hung Lin, Wen-Hao Liu, Haoxing Ren;
     NVIDIA/NYCU; Best Artifact Award). Deep-learning-based component recognition + orientation detection + learned connectivity
     extraction (vs fixed geometric rules). Ships a 100,000-image synthetic schematic dataset for training line detection/interpretation.
     SINA (arXiv 2607.01609 and DATE’26 version arXiv 2601.22114; Aldowaish et al., Fayazi group). Fully automated, open-source,
     handles both IC- and PCB-level. YOLOv11 detection + Connected-Component Labeling (CCL) for connectivity + OCR for
     reference designators + a VLM (GPT-4o) for designator assignment, plus a dedicated crossing-wires detection module
     (morphological transforms + contour analysis) to separate junctions from crossings. Validated with graph isomorphism. Per
     Aldowaish et al., “SINA achieves a component detection precision of 98% for IC-level and 94% for PCB-level circuits, along with a
     text … extraction accuracy of 97.5% … an overall netlist generation accuracy of 96.67%, which is 2.72x higher compared to a state-of-
     the-art open-source method [Masala-CHAI],” and adds a crossing accuracy of 87% with crossing precision of 100%. The detector is
     fine-tuned on >700 annotated schematics. (The DATE’26 version reports the alternate figures: 96.47% overall accuracy, detector F1
     96.47% with precision 94%/recall 99%/weighted mAP 98%.)
     Img2Sim / Img2Sim-V2 (Gurbuz, Balta et al.). CAD tool for user-independent simulation directly from circuit images — an applied
     pipeline in the same family.
     Vision-Based Topology-Consistent Structural Parsing (PMC13259090). Hand-drawn parsing unifying CGHD + Digitize-HCD +
     new samples into a 57-class label system (54 component + text/terminal/crossover). Reports 98.03% component-classification
     accuracy on JUHCCR-v1 (33,329/34,000), beating a 91.15% reference by 6.88 pts, and CGHD mAP@0.5:0.95 under the dataset-paper
     and Bayer-split protocols.
Validation primitives across these: graph isomorphism (SINA, Masala-CHAI), netlist edit distance (Image2Net), and SPICE-simulation
checks (ngspice in the loop).
End-to-end VLM/MLLM methods
Feeding a schematic image to an MLLM and decoding a netlist directly. Consistent empirical finding: off-the-shelf MLLMs are strong at
component perception/classification but weak at full connectivity/netlist reconstruction.
     AMSNet GPT-4/GPT-4o experiments (and the Auto-SPICE/Masala-CHAI case studies) first documented this: GPT-4o mis-lists
     components, confuses NMOS/PMOS, and produces floating nets.
     AMSbench (arXiv 2505.24138; Shi et al.). ~8,000 questions over AMS-Perception (6k), AMS-Analysis (2k), AMS-Design (68),
     evaluating GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Grok-3, Doubao-1.5-vision-pro, Kimi-VL-A3B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (+ DeepSeek-
     R1 on TQA). Per Shi et al., “For component type classification, Gemini performs well, reaching 94% accuracy. Among open-source
     models, Qwen2.5-VL achieves 86%.” But interconnect/netlist recognition lags: topology NED 0.91 (Gemini), 1.40 (GPT-4o), 2.38
     (Qwen2.5-VL); connection-judgment (true/false) ACC reaches ~0.85 for Gemini, yet connection-identification (naming connected
     elements) ACC is only ~0.60 (Gemini), 0.16 (GPT-4o), 0.14 (Qwen2.5-VL). Authors’ verdict: “While the models can produce
     reasonably accurate predictions for local connections, they fall short in reconstructing the complete netlist. Even netlists produced
     by the best-performing model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, require substantial modifications to align with the ground truth.” Root cause: “MLLMs
     are inaccurate in assigning connection points (i.e. pins and ports) to their parent components.” On analysis, the industry-relevant
    trade-off (“Engineer”) sub-task is a low point — “Even the best-performing model, GPT-4o, only achieves 58% accuracy.” On design
    Pass@k, Grok-3/Claude-3.7 lead (Pass@3 up to 0.65), but no model reliably generates syntactically correct testbenches.
    CircuitSense (arXiv 2509.22339; Akbari et al.). 8,006+ problems, six abstraction levels, Perception (890)/Analysis (7,043)/Design
    (157). Headline: “Closed-source models achieve over 85% accuracy on perception tasks … yet their performance on symbolic
    derivation and analytical reasoning falls below 19%.” Gemini-2.5-Pro scores 100% component detection, 100% connection
    identification, 95% function classification on perception. Per Akbari et al., “Gemini-2.5-Pro’s accuracy drops from 80.71% on curated
    multiple-choice problems to 70.32% on open-ended versions, and further collapses to 19.06% on synthetic problems requiring direct
    equation derivation” — an ~61.65-point collapse. Failure-point analysis pins the bottleneck at output-impedance derivation (8%).
    Design accuracy correlates with symbolic ability (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 36.38% schematic-level, 67.27% block-level, 51.35% hierarchical).
    Image2Net (arXiv 2508.13157; Xu et al.; ISEDA 2025). Positions hybrid vs pure-LLM head-to-head and wins: 80.77% success rate,
    +34.62–45.19 pts over prior works; 0.116 average NED, 62.1–69.6% lower than SOTA. Releases device-identification, crossing-
    identification, and device-orientation datasets (2,914 diagrams, 84,195 annotations) plus a 104-pair manually verified eval set and the
    NED metric.
Fine-tuned VLMs and text-side LLMs
    AnalogSeeker (arXiv 2508.10409). Fine-tunes Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct via a “neighborhood self-constrained SFT” (NSC-SFT) that
    adds a KL term anchoring to the base model — an explicit catastrophic-forgetting mitigation. Per the paper, “we train the Qwen2.5-
    32B-Instruct model to obtain AnalogSeeker, which achieves 85.04% accuracy on AMSBench-TQA … with a 15.67% point
    improvement over the original model, and is competitive with mainstream general-purpose LLMs like DeepSeek-v3 and GPT-4o”
    (base 69.37%; QwQ-32B 81.54%; GPT-4o 73.99%; DeepSeek-v3 84.41%). Finding: at textbook-corpus scale, SFT alone beats CPT+SFT
    (<1% delta from adding CPT); “Instruct” models are more robust to fine-tuning than “reasoning” models. Note this is a text-only
    model (AMSBench-TQA is text QA), not a VLM.
    NetlistGPT / ChatSCH (Springer, doi 10.1007/978-981-92-3441-7_32). Board-level, logic-geometry-decoupled: NetlistGPT (fine-
    tuned Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct) does component selection + netlist construction; deterministic algorithms do layout +
    Manhattan routing. Introduces DesignSCH-CoT (3,781 Compact-Netlist CoT samples) and DesignSCH-Bench (232 expert tasks).
    Exemplifies the code-LLM route for the serialization stage.
    LoRA fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-VL / Qwen3-VL / InternVL on schematic data is the natural open-model recipe; DiagramNet-style
    progressive training + decoupled multi-agent workflows report large gains (a 3B model + workflow beating GPT-5/Claude-Sonnet-
    4/Gemini-2.5-Pro by >2× end-to-end on that benchmark, and boosting Gemini-2.5-Pro’s Task-1 performance 128.7×) — evidence that
    domain adaptation + explicit structure closes much of the frontier-model gap.
Agentic / multi-agent frameworks
SPARC (arXiv 2606.20643; Sadia et al.). Multi-agent circuit-diagram QA that grounds reasoning in executable SPICE: a planner
decides #simulations, agents synthesize/repair ngspice programs (patch generation + repair), and an analysis stage interprets
outputs. 83% accuracy, up to +58 pts over baselines. (Distinct from the same-acronym VLM test-time-scaling “Separating
Perception And Reasoning Circuits,” arXiv 2602.06566.)
MuaLLM (arXiv 2508.08137; Fayazi group). Multimodal LLM agent for circuit-design assistance with hybrid contextual RAG.
TopoSizing, HeaRT, SABLE. Netlist-understanding/optimization agents: TopoSizing organizes raw netlists into device-module-
stage hierarchies with LLM hypothesis-verification loops feeding Bayesian optimization; HeaRT is a hierarchical circuit-reasoning-
tree engine for AMS design optimization. These consume netlists (post-recognition) rather than doing recognition, but define the
downstream Pass@k tasks that make recognition worthwhile.

4. Benchmarks & metrics
     Component detection: mAP / mAP@0.5:0.95 (CGHD task, GAT-parsing, Vision-Based Structural Parsing), precision/recall (SINA
     98% IC / 94% PCB).
     Text/designator: OCR accuracy (SINA 97.5%).
     Connectivity/net accuracy: connection judgment ACC + connection identification F1 (AMSbench); link-prediction accuracy (GAT-
     parsing).
     Graph-level: graph isomorphism pass (SINA, Masala-CHAI), graph edit distance; Netlist Edit Distance (NED) — Image2Net’s
     contribution, computed on the heterogeneous graph, and adopted as “Topology NED” in AMSbench.
     Netlist exact-match / success rate: Image2Net 80.77% success; SINA 96.67%.
     SPICE-simulation equivalence: ngspice execution in Masala-CHAI, SPARC.
     Downstream Pass@k: Masala-CHAI (fine-tuned models >40% Pass@1 on complex circuits where base ≈0); AMSbench design
     Pass@3/5/10; CircuitSense design accuracy.
The perception-vs-connectivity gap, quantified. AMSbench: 94% component-type classification vs 0.91 topology NED and ~0.60
connection-identification (Gemini-2.5-Pro), collapsing to 0.14–0.16 for GPT-4o/Qwen2.5-VL. CircuitSense: >85% perception vs 19.06%
synthetic equation derivation (80.71→19.06, ~61-point drop for the best model). This is the exact structural signature the Document-AI
reader knows from table structure recognition: local cell/component detection is easy; global adjacency/reading-order/net reconstruction
is where systems fail, and it is where explicit structural modeling (GNN link prediction, CCL, graph-iso validation) outperforms end-to-
end decoding.
5. Parallels to document structure recognition
     Component detection ≈ cell/word detection. Same detectors (YOLO, Faster R-CNN, DETR-family, Swin), same long-tail-class and
     small-object problems; textbook uniformity is exploited exactly as in born-digital document harvesting.
     Net reconstruction ≈ table structure / adjacency recovery. Predicting which pins share a net is isomorphic to predicting which cells
     share a row/column — hence GAT link-prediction and heterogeneous-graph formulations. Endpoint snapping / junction detection ≈
     ruling-line and cell-boundary reconstruction.
     Crossing-vs-junction disambiguation ≈ spanning-cell / merged-cell ambiguity — a local visual cue (junction dot vs hop) with
     global topological consequences, which is why SINA and Image2Net build dedicated crossing modules (SINA reports 87% crossing
     accuracy / 100% crossing precision).
     The perception-vs-global-structure gap is the shared failure mode; the field’s response (hybrid perception + explicit structure +
     LLM repair) mirrors the trajectory of table recognition away from pure end-to-end image-to-markup.
     Synthetic data generation — rendering schematics from SPICE (Netlistify’s 100k, CircuitSense’s grid-based generator with SymPy
     ground-truth) is the direct analog of programmatic table/document synthesis.
Recommendations
Staged build recipe (highest expected accuracy today):
 1. Perception stage. Fine-tune a strong detector (YOLOv11 as in SINA, or a DETR/Swin variant per GAT-parsing) on CGHD + Digitize-
    HCD + Image2Net device sets for components, junctions, wire-hops/crossings, and text regions. Target mAP/precision >95% on
    printed, and expect a domain gap on hand-drawn — train per-regime or mix. Benchmark: if component precision <90%, connectivity
    cannot recover; fix perception first.
 2. Connection points + net reconstruction. Add explicit port/pin localization (port-heatmaps as in Digitize-HCD; port-coordinate
    module as in GAT-parsing) and reconstruct nets deterministically via CCL + Hough with explicit crossing-vs-junction
    disambiguation (morphological/contour, SINA-style). This is the single highest-leverage stage — it is where end-to-end MLLMs fail.
 3. Serialization + repair. Use a fine-tuned code-LLM (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B/32B, cf. NetlistGPT) or VLM (Qwen2.5-VL / Qwen3-VL,
    InternVL) to assign reference designators/values and emit SPICE, with the schematic image as visual context (SINA-style VLM
    designator assignment). Prefer a compact-netlist DSL intermediate (ChatSCH-style, ~85% token reduction) for long circuits, then
    expand to full SPICE.
 4. Validation loop. Verify with graph isomorphism against the reconstructed graph and, where component values exist, ngspice
    simulation-equivalence (SPARC/Masala-CHAI closed loop). Use NED (Image2Net) as the continuous training/eval signal rather
    than brittle exact-match.
When to consider pure end-to-end VLM: only for perception-heavy sub-tasks (component listing/classification, captioning, QA) or as a
repair/serialization module — not for standalone full-netlist extraction. The benchmarks are unambiguous that this underperforms
hybrids today.
Serving with vLLM for bulk conversion + synthetic-data generation:
     Serve Qwen2.5-VL/Qwen3-VL or InternVL with vllm serve , using tensor/data parallelism ( --tensor-parallel-size , --data-
     parallel-size ), --limit-mm-per-prompt to cap image inputs, --max-model-len sized to your longest netlist (native 128K; 64K
     typical), and --mm-encoder-tp-mode data to replicate the small ViT encoder (ViT ≈ 675M vs 72B LM). Use --gpu-memory-
     utilization 0.95 to maximize KV cache; on A100-80GB, TP≥2 for the 72B to avoid OOM.
     Exploit vLLM guided/structured decoding (guided_json / grammar) to constrain output to a netlist grammar — this directly attacks
     format-invalid outputs (the open-source-model failure noted in AMSbench, where some models “fail to produce outputs in the
     correct format”) and reduces repair cycles.
     For synthetic-data generation at scale, render schematics from a SPICE/topology corpus (AnalogGenie topologies, OCB, CktGNN
     generators; Netlistify’s 100k pipeline; CircuitSense’s grid generator with SymPy ground-truth) and use the vLLM-served VLM for
     augmentation/captioning. Textbook harvesting (AMSNet/Masala-CHAI YOLO-on-PDF) remains the best source of realistic printed
     styles.
Open-model choices. Qwen3-VL (multiple sizes incl. 235B-A22B MoE) and InternVL3 for perception; Qwen2.5-Coder-7B/32B for
serialization/repair; 3–7B fine-tunes are sufficient when paired with an explicit structural stage (DiagramNet-style 3B beating frontier
models end-to-end with the right workflow).
Metric thresholds that should change your plan: if topology NED plateaus >0.3 or graph-iso pass-rate <80% after detector
improvements, the bottleneck is net reconstruction/crossing disambiguation, not perception — invest there. If designator/value errors
dominate, add OCR + VLM cross-checking (SINA). If long circuits fail disproportionately, move to a compact DSL and chunk.
Caveats
     Reported numbers are authors’ own, mostly on self-constructed benchmarks and largely unaudited (AMSbench, CircuitSense,
     SINA, Image2Net are arXiv preprints; cross-comparability is limited because NED, success-rate, and iso-pass are defined per-paper).
     SINA’s two versions report 96.67% vs 96.47% overall accuracy — minor version drift.
     CircuitSense preprint has internal naming inconsistencies (Gemini-2.0 vs 2.5-Pro; Claude-Sonnet-3.5 vs 4; a Table-7 percentage
     mismatch on total-impedance vs transfer-function-simplification); treat the best model as Gemini-2.5-Pro per its appendix version
     spec ( gemini-2.5-pro-preview-0605 ).
